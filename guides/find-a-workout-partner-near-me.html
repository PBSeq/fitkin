How to find a workout partner near you (who actually shows up)

Most people don't struggle to find someone to work out with. They struggle to find someone whose schedule, level, and location line up week after week. Here's how to get that right.

1. Decide what "partner" means for you

Before you look, write down three things. It takes two minutes and it filters out most mismatches:

  • The activity. Running, lifting, climbing, tennis, swimming — or several.
  • When you can really go. Not when you wish you could. "Mon/Wed/Fri at 6am" is a real answer; "whenever" isn't.
  • Your honest level. A beginner paired with someone training for a marathon rarely lasts. Similar pace keeps both people coming back.

2. Where to look

  • Run clubs and group runs. Many running stores and breweries host free weekly group runs. Show up twice and you'll know who runs your pace.
  • Your own gym. Regulars at the same hour are already on your schedule. Front-desk staff and trainers often know who's looking for a partner.
  • Climbing gyms and tennis courts. Both are partner sports by nature. Climbing gyms often run belay-partner meetups; courts often have ladders or drop-in nights.
  • Meetup groups and community boards. Search your city plus the activity. Check how recently the group actually met.
  • Apps built for workout partners. Useful when you want to filter by schedule and level before meeting anyone.

3. Pick for consistency, not chemistry

The best partner isn't the most fun person — it's the one who shares your time slot and pace. Before committing, check:

  • Do at least two of your available days overlap?
  • Is their level within one step of yours?
  • Is the meeting spot reasonably close for both of you?

4. Make the first session easy (and safe)

  • Meet somewhere public and familiar — a gym, a busy trail head, a court.
  • Keep the first session short and low-stakes. An easy run or a light workout.
  • Tell a friend where you're going.
  • Agree on the next session before you leave. The second meetup is where partnerships stick or fade.

5. Where fitkin fits in

We built fitkin because the "schedule, level, location" match was the hard part for us too. Here's exactly what it does:

  • You make a short card: your sports, when you usually train, your level (day one → beast mode), and your ZIP.
  • It shows people nearby, sorted by what you share — sports, days, time of day, and level.
  • You tap 👋 wave on someone. Nothing opens until they wave back. Then you're "kin" and can message.
  • Your ZIP (or an optional rough ~5 km area computed on your phone) is used for matching — your exact location never leaves your device.
  • Block and report are built in. It's not a dating app.

It's new and still small, so in many cities you may be one of the first people there. Matching and messaging are free.
